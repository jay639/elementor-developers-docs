Where the leaders are weak

  • Size and bloat. 150–265 MB base APKs, 10–15 code files each, mostly ad SDKs. Slow installs, more crashes and data use on budget phones. We are 30 MB.
  • Ad pressure. Each of the top three runs 20+ ad networks, full-screen offers and fail offers. App 3 adds lives gating and a battle pass on top. Players who want calm puzzles feel squeezed.
  • Feature sprawl. App 2 has tarot, zodiac, chess, shark and cat mini-modes beside the core game. It earns but dilutes the identity.
  • No accessibility. No sign of colorblind, high-contrast or screen-reader support in any of the four.
  • Online dependence. App 1's daily challenge has to download; bundles and configs are remote in 2 and 3.
  • App 4 has nice ideas (voice praise, sound packs, named mazes) but no tutorial, so new players churn.

Next step outside this analysis: read their 1–2 star Play reviews to confirm which of these players complain about most.

What to build, in order

P0 · Fix the core feel (do first)

1. Long, thin, winding arrows

Change the generator so arrows are 3–15 cells that bend, packed to fill the board shape, and draw them as thin rounded lines (about 0.12–0.16 of a cell) with a compact head, over a soft dot grid. Keep the current "thick arrows" setting as an accessibility option.

Where: generator.dart (body growth, target length), board_painter.dart (stroke, head, dot grid). Seen in: 1, 2, 3, 4.

2. A real difficulty curve with visible tiers

Grow boards much faster (about 6×6 by level 10, 10×10 by level 30, 20×20+ late with zoom), target deeper chains, and fewer free moves at the start. Tag levels Normal, Hard and Super Hard with a pre-level banner and bigger rewards; keep a breather after each Hard.

Where: campaign.dart curve and size caps, analyzer.dart score. Seen in: 2 (Normal/Hard/Super hard), 3 (pre-level screen), 1 (custom difficulties).

3. First-time tutorial with a hand

Level 1: dim the board, spotlight one free arrow, animate a hand tapping it. Then show a blocked arrow bump with "Clear the way first". On the first big board, show a two-finger pinch animation. Introduce each mechanic (trucks, lights, gates, ambulance) with a one-screen animated popup the first time it appears.

Seen in: 1 (onboarding, "Tap an arrow to remove!", "Pinch to zoom"), 2 (guide hand, guide video, one-hand-scale), 3 (tutorial mask).

P1 · Make every level feel rewarding

4. Combo and praise

Quick consecutive correct taps build a combo with rising pitch and a short praise ("Nice!", "Brilliant!"), optional voice lines, and small coin bonuses. Encouragement pops at 25%, 75% and 90% cleared.

Seen in: 3 (combo with decay and rewards), 2 (encourage at 25/75/90%), 4 (17 voice lines).

5. Juice

Gradient trail on exiting arrows, small spark when an arrow leaves, board-appear animation, confetti and a picture-reveal moment on win (the shape fills in as arrows clear).

Seen in: 1 (gradient trail, solved-dots reveal, board appear), 3 (spark particles, hit impact).

6. Boosters that fit our mechanics

Eraser (remove one arrow), Hint (exists), Grid Guide (show paths for one level; keep "show paths" free in accessibility settings), Undo (exists). Earn by coins, video or daily reward.

Seen in: 1 (Hint, Eraser, Magic Wand, Grid Guide with tutorials).

P2 · Retention and live-ops

7. Daily challenge calendar and monthly trophy

Show a month grid; completing every day earns a collectible trophy. Our daily is already offline and deterministic, which is a plus.

8. Collections built on what we have

Name each shape board (we already have cars, animals, landmarks) and collect them into an album; let city blocks unlock themed districts. Weekly themed events with a small leaderboard come after.

9. Re-engagement

Local push notifications (daily ready, streak at risk), a rate prompt after a win streak, a returning-player gift, and a second-chance offer on fail (+1 heart for a video, already built) tuned by Remote Config.

10. Sound

Add soft background music with its own toggle and two or three selectable sound packs.

Seen in: 2 (BGM bundles), 4 (Classic Arcade, Deep Basswave, Zen Chimes, Cyber Laser).

Keep as differentiators

  • Small download, offline play, fair ad frequency, and accessibility modes. None of the four have all of these.
  • The city map and skins: you said they look good, and only one competitor has anything like a growing world.

How this was done

From the four base APKs: decoded manifests, SDK packages in the code, Unity IL2CPP metadata (class names) for apps 1 and 3, asset-bundle names for app 2, and the JavaScript bundle for app 4. App 2's metadata and bundles, and app 3's level files, are encrypted, so their exact level layouts were not readable. No competitor code, art or level data was copied into our app; this is design research only.
